Gemini 4 Neon released for engineering, knowledge work and science

19 Nov 2026 · 3 picks

Google DeepMind's Gemini 4 Neon model is now available, with a 1 million-token context window and four scalable thinking levels. It ranks highly on several benchmarks, and its pricing is competitive.

  1. Introducing Gemini 4 Neon

    Google DeepMind · 18 Nov 2026

    Google DeepMind released Gemini 4 Neon, a model designed for software engineering, knowledge work, and science workflows. It features a 1 million-token context window and four scalable thinking levels. According to Google, it ranks #3 overall on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index with a score of 61 at the xhigh thinking level, #2 on the Vals Index, and #1 on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index with a score of 58.6. It also achieved top rankings on the Legal Research Bench, Tax Agent Bench, and Zapier’s AutomationBench with 56.3%. In cyber defense, it reached 76% on the hard tier of AutoBaxBench and 51.26% on WebExploitBench v1.2. The pricing is $3 per million input tokens, $0.15 per million cached input tokens, and $15 per million output tokens. This model could significantly improve the efficiency of coding tasks and knowledge work. I would check how it handles complex, multi-step engineering problems and whether its performance metrics translate to real-world benefits, especially in regulated environments like banking.

  2. How Airbnb uses cost per task to operate coding agents on Amazon Bedrock

    AWS Machine Learning · 18 Nov 2026

    Airbnb uses Amazon Bedrock for its internal tool AirChat, which runs coding agents. According to AWS, AirChat processes about two thirds of requests through new models by mid-October. Amazon Bedrock provides runtime metrics in Amazon CloudWatch, which help investigate latency increases, throttling, and changes in token consumption. This could be a strong example of how to manage AI workloads at scale.

  3. Copilot is "the new OS for work," and now Office runs inside of it

    Ars Technica · 18 Nov 2026

    Microsoft announced the general availability of Word, Excel, and PowerPoint inside Copilot, describing it as the new OS for work. Copilot Autopilot is a persistent, proactive agent for personal productivity that uses usage-based billing via Copilot Credits. Work IQ is a context layer that helps agents understand a company’s communications, relationships, and ongoing work. This integration could streamline workflows, but I would evaluate how well it handles complex, multi-service tasks.

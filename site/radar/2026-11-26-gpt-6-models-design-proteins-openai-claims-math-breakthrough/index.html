GPT-6 models design proteins, OpenAI claims math breakthrough

26 Nov 2026 · 2 picks

OpenAI's GPT-6 models show new capabilities in protein design and math problem-solving, while Anthropic tests Claude's robustness.

  1. GPT-6 models demonstrate intrinsic protein-design capabilities

    OpenAI · 24 Nov 2026

    OpenAI's GPT-6 models have demonstrated the ability to design new protein shapes and sequences using reasoning and code, without relying on specialized biology models. According to OpenAI, an internal research model matched or outperformed specialized models on many protein design tasks. In lab tests with Onava, proteins based on GPT-6.2’s designs showed evidence of binding across five targets. The models’ written reasoning and code suggest they draw on math, physics, chemistry, and biological knowledge. This development is significant for backend teams working in biotech or healthcare, as it opens new avenues for AI-assisted protein engineering. I would verify how these models integrate with existing bioinformatics tools and whether they can handle the scale and complexity of real-world protein design tasks.

  2. Anthropic set more than 1,700 traps for Claude — what it found is both reassuring and unsettling

    The New Stack · 25 Nov 2026

    Anthropic tested Claude's behavior with a variety of traps, finding that it is relatively robust against jailbreaking attempts. According to The New Stack, only a small number of traps were successful in causing Claude to produce unwanted outputs. This is reassuring for teams deploying AI models in sensitive environments, as it shows that Claude can be made more secure.

Anthropic's Claude designed protein binders against scorpion neurotoxins, and Cloudflare introduced tsrs, a TypeScript compiler ported to Rust.

  1. How Claude designed protein binders against scorpion neurotoxins

    Anthropic Research · 20 Oct 2026

    Anthropic's Claude designed 211 minibinders against five scorpion neurotoxins from four species. Out of these, 17 designs met the criteria for binding, according to Anthropic Research. The designs were lab-tested by Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience. This breakthrough in protein design could lead to new treatments for scorpion stings, which affect thousands of people annually. I would verify the binding efficacy and safety in a cellular environment before considering it for clinical use. Additionally, I would check how this technology could be adapted for other venomous species, potentially saving more lives. The ability to design protein binders with AI could revolutionize the field of toxicology and lead to more effective treatments for various venomous bites and stings.

  2. Introducing tsrs: A port of TypeScript to Rust

    Cloudflare · 20 Oct 2026

    Cloudflare, VoidZero, and Max Schwenk of Owner.com are collaborating on tsrs, a Rust port of the TypeScript compiler. Early benchmarks show tsrs is up to 8x faster and uses ~70% less memory compared to TypeScript 7.0. The Rust + WebAssembly story offers unique advantages for memory efficiency. This could be a game-changer for performance-critical applications.

  3. 8 eMudhra certificates are compromised. Here's what you need to know

    Cloudflare · 20 Oct 2026

    Cloudflare discovered that 8 eMudhra certificates were compromised, potentially allowing attackers to create forged certificates. Cloudflare has released updates to protect against this issue. I would ensure that all certificates in use are up-to-date and monitor for any signs of exploitation.

  1. Co-Designing Sparse Attention for Efficient Long-Context Inference

    NVIDIA Developer · 17 Nov 2026

    NVIDIA has published a detailed approach to co-designing sparse attention for efficient long-context inference. Sparse attention limits each query to a small subset of keys, shifting the cost of scoring all keys into an indexer. This indexer operates as a batched matmul with a fused epilogue, and its arithmetic intensity scales with group size and speculative draft length. According to NVIDIA, using a key head dimension of 128 or 256 balances Tensor Core utilization against decode memory traffic. This optimization is crucial for backend teams managing large-scale AI models, as it can significantly reduce the computational overhead. I would verify the compatibility of these optimizations with existing infrastructure and assess the potential performance gains before adopting them. Reducing the number of keys scored through compression or hierarchical indexing further cuts work in both the indexer and Top-k selection, which is a practical consideration for teams looking to optimize their AI workflows.

  2. Nvidia’s Board Has No Plans to Bring Back Jensen Huang

    Wired · 17 Nov 2026

    Nvidia reported $115.8 billion in revenue for the quarter that ended on October 25, with its data center business generating $107.6 billion, up 110% from a year ago. Nvidia expects to keep growing throughout 2027 and into 2028, fueled by the rise of agentic AI.

  3. Muse Is Now Available in Australia: The World’s First Personal AI Agent Built for Everyone

    Meta · 17 Nov 2026

    Meta's personal AI agent Muse is now available in Australia, offering a range of tasks and projects across multiple platforms. Muse can connect to various apps and services, providing a seamless user experience. The feature called Spaces allows for custom experiences, and Muse Secure VM ensures secure data storage. This expansion is worth watching for backend engineers looking to integrate AI agents into their systems.

  4. How Rakbank built centralized observability at scale with Amazon OpenSearch Service

    AWS Architecture · 17 Nov 2026

    According to AWS Architecture, Rakbank's retail digital banking platform ingests 150 GB of telemetry per day, running as containerized microservices on Amazon EKS. The platform retains 9 TB of logs in OpenSearch Service over a 60-day window and archives approximately 54 TB of log data per year. This centralized observability setup is a strong example of scalable log management, which is relevant for high-traffic, regulated environments.

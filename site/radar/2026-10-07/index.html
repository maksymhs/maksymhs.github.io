Grab halves p99 latency by redesigning its counter storage

7 Oct 2026 · 2 picks

A careful storage migration at about a billion requests a day: an abstraction layer, shadow traffic and a data model that needs fewer round trips.

  1. Grab Redesigns Counter Service Storage for 50% Lower P99 Latency

    InfoQ · 7 Oct 2026

    Grab's Counter Service feeds its anti-fraud platform and handles about one billion requests per day. It moved from a wide-column database to Aerospike and, more interestingly, changed the data model: 15-minute, hourly and daily counts were separate rows, costing four network round trips per event, and now live in one record per counter with a timestamp-ordered map. Grab reports roughly 50% lower production p99 read latency, data down from about 3 TB to 1 TB and 45% to 50% lower cost per node. Fewer round trips is also what made the aggregation endpoints I built at Simyo twice as fast. What I like most is the rollout: a storage facade with legacy, Aerospike and mock implementations, shadow reads ramped from 5% to 100%, and parity checked before live traffic moved. Prove equivalence with real traffic before you cut over.

  2. Spanner Omni Reaches GA, Replacing Google's Atomic Clocks and File System with Software

    InfoQ · 7 Oct 2026

    Google made Spanner Omni generally available: the distributed SQL database can now run on-premises, across clouds or on a laptop, replacing Colossus and TrueTime with software equivalents. The article notes there is no availability SLA, so I would check what that means before putting it near a payments path.

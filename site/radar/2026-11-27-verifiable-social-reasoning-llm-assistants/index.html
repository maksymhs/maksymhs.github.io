  1. Verifiable Social Reasoning for LLM Assistants

    Google Research · 25 Nov 2026

    According to Google Research: Zorik Gekhman and Amir Taubenfeld of Google Research introduced an evaluation framework for AI assistants' social reasoning. The framework uses multi-agent social simulations to quantify how well AI assistants understand social situations from users' subjective narratives. A target agent with a hidden motive interacts with other agents, including one representing the user, who then consults the evaluated assistant to infer the target’s motive. The framework addresses challenges in evaluating social reasoning, including the lack of verifiable ground truth for social properties like intentions. Evaluating diverse LLMs through the framework showed that subjective user mediation complicates social reasoning, and models are sensitive to user framing. Models require more detail than humans to make correct predictions and fail to fully leverage clarification questions.

Disney's $12.5M deal for Character.AI tech avoids lawsuits

19 Oct 2026 · 4 picks

Disney's acquisition of Character.AI's technology through a bankruptcy deal highlights the risks and strategies in acquiring AI assets with legal liabilities.

  1. Disney found a way to buy Character.AI without buying its lawsuits

    Ars Technica · 19 Oct 2026

    Disney bought Character.AI's technology for $5.5 million plus a $2 million deposit in September, and aims to pay an additional $12.5 million for a perpetual license. According to Ars Technica, Character.AI declared bankruptcy on October 12, facing multiple lawsuits over safety issues with its chatbots. The deal does not include a merger, and Character.AI remains responsible for its legal liabilities. This strategy allows Disney to acquire valuable AI technology while avoiding the legal risks associated with Character.AI's ongoing lawsuits. It is a smart move for companies looking to acquire AI assets without inheriting legal baggage. I would assess how Disney plans to integrate this technology into its existing systems and how it will ensure the safety and reliability of the AI chatbots.

    InfoQ · 19 Oct 2026

    Pinterest's use of event time watermarks to finalize petabyte-scale data partitions is a significant advancement in data ingestion frameworks. According to InfoQ, this mechanism allows downstream jobs to determine when streaming data is sufficiently complete to consume, addressing challenges in continuous Change Data Capture (CDC) ingestion. This approach could be valuable for backend teams dealing with large-scale data processing.

Powered by Playwright Workspaces, this tool supports interactions with both public websites and private applications. This tool could streamline browser-based tasks and improve automation workflows.

    Meta Engineering · 19 Oct 2026
